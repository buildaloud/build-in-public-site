# Restatement & throat-clear patterns

### callback-restatement (2026-07-14)
Watch for a specific restatement pattern: a factual claim stated once mid-post (e.g. "round cap halts and drops blockers on my desk", "orchestrator owns topic-approval + assembly + commit") gets restated near-verbatim in a later beat that explicitly signals it's a callback ("the same split from earlier," "same math as the hook"). The signaling sentence itself is fine (intentional bookend); the literal re-statement of specifics right after it is the empty one — gate the restatement, not the callback line. Also watch definition paragraphs (e.g. paraphrasing a cited policy) for a filler middle sentence that just re-says the clause right before it in fewer words ("The policy targets intent to manipulate rankings." added nothing beyond the "primary purpose of manipulating search rankings" clause one sentence earlier) — these read as summary sentences but carry zero new info.

### turn-beat-throat-clear (2026-07-15)
Real catch on `automate-blog-writing-with-ai-agents`: even in this heavily fact-cited pipeline, a "turn"-formula beat (paragraphFormula turn, e.g. "This post broke the machine that wrote it") can still open with a vague meta-commentary throat-clear that isn't in the outline's intendedBeat/ourTake — e.g. "Everything above sounds tidy." before the actual concrete reveal. This is drafter-added decoration, not outline-mandated, and it's the exact "beat-opening throat-clear" pattern named in the agent spec. Gate it; cut the throat-clear sentence and start straight on the concrete claim.

### attribution-throat-clear (2026-07-15)
Real catch on `writing-alt-text-seo-accessibility`: a standalone attribution sentence that announces a source is about to make a point ("Google's own image SEO guidance calls that out by name.") immediately followed by the actual quoted point as its own sentence is a gateable throat-clear — the fix is merging attribution + quote into one sentence via a colon (a pattern already used elsewhere in the same draft, e.g. "Here's the real alt text sitting on... right now:"). Same pattern, different shape: a bare judgment-label sentence stated before its own evidence ("That's the image described honestly." then, as a separate sentence, the reason why) — merge via em-dash into the evidence sentence rather than leaving the label to stand alone.

### protected-clause-scope (2026-07-15)
When an outline's `gateGuidance` protects one specific clause verbatim inside a beat (e.g. "keep the ourTake's opening line X intact"), only that clause is exempt from flatness scrutiny — sentences the drafter added around it are still fair game. Caught in `writing-alt-text-seo-accessibility`: the protected clause "How to write alt text for SEO comes down to sequencing" was followed by two drafter-added sentences that just re-stated the rule already given in full two sentences earlier in the same beat ("Description first, keyword second. The keyword has to earn its place inside a description that was already true without it.") — gated the trailing restatement, kept the protected opening.

### meta-commentary-fix (2026-07-15)
Watch the synthesis's fix to a gated restatement (protected-clause-scope entry above): the redraft swapped the restated rule for self-referential meta-commentary about the article's own SEO/search placement — "Same rule as two paragraphs up, addressed to whoever searched this exact phrase." That's a worse empty than the original: it says nothing about the topic and breaks voice by narrating the post's own keyword strategy to the reader. When a restatement fix trades content-restatement for structure-commentary ("same rule as above," "addressed to whoever searched X"), gate the meta-commentary too — the fix is to delete it outright and let the protected clause stand alone as a short one-sentence paragraph, not to patch in a new sentence.

### explain-after-punchline (2026-07-15)
New recurring pattern on `writing-alt-text-seo-accessibility`: when a beat's `gateGuidance` explicitly instructs "state X, then resist the urge to explain it further / let the reader hear it," the drafter still tacks on an explanatory clause after the punchline (e.g. "Read both strings out loud and the argument settles itself: [then a full clause re-explaining that description-first serves both listener and crawler]" — a restatement of the post's own thesis already established several times over). The explanation clause is the gate target even though the setup sentence obeys the instruction; cut the trailing clause and let the stated punchline stand alone. Watch for this whenever gateGuidance contains "let it speak for itself" / "resist explaining" language paired with a colon construction in the draft.

### negated-restatement (2026-07-15)
A negated-restatement sentence that re-asserts the same claim as the sentence before it by negating the actor instead of the state (e.g. "Filler sinks toward the tail on its own. No one has to push it there." — the second sentence adds zero beyond "on its own" already said) is still an empty sentence even though it isn't a verbatim echo. Gate and delete the negated tail, keep the original claim.

### two-facts-label (2026-07-16)
"Meta-commentary about two facts just given": after stating two separate sourced quotes/facts back to back, a bare sentence just labeling them as separate ("Two independent facts, standing on their own.") adds nothing — the reader already sees they're two sentences with two different attributions. Same species as meta-commentary-fix above but applied to facts instead of the post's own SEO placement. Gate and delete; the separateness is already visible from the prose structure, it doesn't need to be announced.

### numeric-echo (2026-07-16)
"Numeric-echo before an enumeration": a bare fragment restating a count that was already stated one sentence earlier, placed right before a list that enumerates that exact count ("The stance baked into the pipeline is precision over recall... Six lanes, six jobs. A surface-mapper only enumerates..." — "six lanes" was never stated before this exact point, but the fragment adds nothing the enumeration itself doesn't already prove; it's a transition-throat-clear, not new information). Gate and cut; let the list follow directly from the preceding sentence.

### strawman-confirm (2026-07-16)
`hired-a-team-of-specialists`: after stating a sourced statistic, a two-fragment beat ("Not a hunch. Anthropic measured it.") sets up a contrast nobody raised (nothing called it a hunch) just to knock it down — the "measured it" half is already implied by "backs that up with a number" one clause earlier, so the pair adds zero new information despite reading as punchy. Gate; replace with the bare attribution alone ("Anthropic measured it.") and drop the strawman half.

### colon-split-throat-clear (2026-07-16)
New recurring pattern, confirmed 4x in one draft on `rate-limiting-an-llm-so-a-stranger-cant-run-up-my-bill`: when an outline's `intendedBeat` merges a vague label clause and its concrete cash-out into ONE sentence via a colon (e.g. "...narrower cousin of the same class: a public endpoint plus a metered model..."), check whether the draft split that colon into two separate sentences — the first half then reads as a bare, vague preamble with zero information of its own until the second sentence supplies it. Hits found: "What's left is a narrower cousin of the same failure class." + "A public endpoint plus a metered model produces the same climbing number..."; "Next to that number, the math behind this box looks almost quaint." + the Haiku/Vercel Gateway specifics; "That removes a whole class of failure before it can happen." + "There's no reset job to write, and no chance of one breaking silently..."; "One honest asterisk covers every counter above." + "Usage gets recorded only once a reply finishes..."; and one not outline-colon-sourced but same shape, "An LLM breaks that model." + "The unit of cost is the token...". Gate and merge back into one sentence via colon (or em-dash) — same fix every time; check this pattern first-pass by diffing draft sentence breaks against any colon in the outline's `intendedBeat`. NOT this pattern: a general claim followed by TWO OR MORE distinct concrete examples (not one direct single cash-out) reads as legitimate topic-sentence-then-examples — don't gate that shape (e.g. "Every single limit is beatable on its own." followed by two separate cap-evasion examples is fine).
