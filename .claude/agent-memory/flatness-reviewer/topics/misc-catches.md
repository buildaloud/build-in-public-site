# Misc one-off catches and general notes

### outline-vs-draft (2026-07-14)
This pipeline's outlines (e.g. `automate-blog-writing-with-ai-agents`) tend to carry heavily fact-cited, numerically concrete `goal`/`ourTake`/`intendedBeat` fields by construction (facts arrays with named sources back nearly every claim). Outline-stage flatness gates are rare here — the real empties, when they happen, show up in DRAFT mode where prose flourishes get added around the sourced claims. Don't expect to gate outlines from this pipeline often; save scrutiny for draft-mode review.

### orphaned-number (2026-07-15)
When a draft unpacks a bulleted score/data list in prose right after (walking through each number one at a time), watch for one item in the walk-through getting a bare restatement of its number with no interpretation while its siblings each add a reading of theirs (e.g. "Malicious intent is a flat zero, [and means X]... Overall exposure sits at 4.6 — [low, meaning Y]... Misuse surface is 85." — the third just re-announces a number already in the list two lines up, no clause explaining what it means). Gate: give the orphaned number the same interpretive clause its siblings got.

### missing-code-block (2026-07-15)
Real catch on `design-system-with-css-variables`: when a fenced-code-block beat (`rendersAsProse: false`, e.g. the real token-block excerpt) is entirely absent from a draft — its content folded piecemeal into later prose beats instead — treat that as a gate-level flatness finding, not just a structure note: the beat's whole job (show the concrete artifact) delivers zero content, the most extreme form of an off-guidance beat. Fix is insert-after with the actual code block built from the outline's facts array (verbatim hexes/names), not a request to "add it back."

### missing-paragraph-break (2026-07-15)
Also on `design-system-with-css-variables`: a `gateGuidance` instruction to "give this confession its own paragraph break" is a paragraph-formula/structure note, but when the drafter ignores it and keeps everything as one long undifferentiated paragraph, the turn's closing line reads more like a tidy-bow restatement than a landed beat even though its content is technically non-empty — flag the missing paragraph break as an elevation (not gate) tied to the flatness risk it creates. See `tidy-bow-closers.md` for the separate gate-level closer finding in the same run.

### universality-tail (2026-07-15)
Real catch on `which-claude-model-to-use`: this drafter has a recurring tic of tacking a decorative absolute-emphasis tail onto a sentence that already established universality/exceptionlessness, adding zero new info: a standalone sentence ("...pays that multiplier on every call, whether the call needed it or not. No exceptions, no matter the task.") and a comma-spliced tail ("Every marketplace audit since has run on Sonnet, no exceptions."). Gate both shapes — the standalone throwaway sentence and the comma-tail clause — whenever "no exceptions" / "every time" / "nothing more" restates a universality claim already made by "every" or "whether X or not" one clause earlier. Fix: delete the standalone sentence outright; for the comma-tail, cut back to the plain sentence.

### outline-arithmetic (2026-07-15)
Real outline-stage catch on `ai-automation-stack` (rarer than draft-stage per `outline-vs-draft` above, but happens): an `ourTake` field can contain a clever-sounding enumerative/arithmetic turn of phrase that doesn't actually parse against the post's own stated facts — e.g. "A measurement layer that flatters you is a fifth way each of the four layers above can quietly go wrong" when the post's own `point` field defines measurement as one of exactly four layers, not a separate fifth. The "fifth way" framing is decoration reaching for cleverness that collapses under a beat of scrutiny — gate it even at outline stage since a confusing `ourTake` will draft into confused or empty prose. Fix: replace with a plain causal claim that keeps the real idea (a lying measurement layer hides problems in the other layers) without the arithmetic that doesn't check out.

### link-anchor-redundancy (2026-07-16)
New pattern on `hired-a-team-of-specialists` hook beat: a required markdown-link anchor text forces a restatement. When a fact needs a specific link (e.g. anchor text "Claude Code ships subagents by default") and the drafter also writes a preceding plain sentence carrying the same claim in different words ("Subagents ship as part of Claude Code's own config, not a premium tier gated behind a paywall."), the plain sentence is the empty one — gate it and merge: drop the standalone lead sentence, open directly on the linked sentence, and fold any unique clause from the dropped sentence (here, "not a premium tier gated behind a paywall") into the linked sentence as a modifier so nothing is lost except the duplicated claim.

### outline-intendedbeat-insertion (2026-07-16)
Technique confirmed on `rate-limiting-an-llm-so-a-stranger-cant-run-up-my-bill`: when a beat's `intendedBeat` text is prose-complete (reads like near-final copy), diff the draft sentence-by-sentence against it. A short punchy sentence the drafter added beyond what `intendedBeat` specifies is a prime empty candidate — e.g. draft added "Nothing stopped it." right after "No bug was involved. The service ran exactly as designed, fast and unattended." even though the outline's `intendedBeat` goes straight from that line to the next beat's content. The extra sentence just re-announces "unattended" in dramatic form. Not every outline-absent sentence is empty (drafters legitimately add connective tissue), but it's a fast first pass: check insertions against `intendedBeat` before judging on vibes alone.

### unsupported-abstraction (2026-07-16)
New pattern, "unsupported process abstraction": a sentence describing pipeline mechanics that isn't grounded in any outline `facts` entry and is vague enough that its own meaning is unclear on a re-read — e.g. "No reviewer overrides the next check." dropped into a beat about reviewers each grading one axis, with no fact backing what "overrides the next check" actually means mechanically. Unlike the sourced/numeric claims this pipeline's drafts usually carry (see `outline-vs-draft` above), an invented mechanic sentence with no outline support is a flag on sight — gate and delete rather than try to guess and repair the intended meaning.
