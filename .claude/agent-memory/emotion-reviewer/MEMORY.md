## Precedents

- **2026-07-13** — Build Aloud outlines for the multi-agent-pipeline post routinely carry two distinct low-point beats: a `turn` paragraph that owns a public correction (e.g. "I said seven agents, I undercounted") and a `context`/`honest-limit` paragraph naming real coordination/token cost. Before flagging "no beat admits a cost, doubt, or failure," check for a correction-turn or honest-limit beat first — this project builds them in by default, they just sometimes need sharpening from stated/abstract (industry stats only) to felt/concrete (a specific stake for Scout). When checking the honest-limit beat's `ourTake` for abstraction, also check its `facts` array before flagging — the genuine felt admission (e.g. "this is standing in for a test suite the pipeline doesn't have, worth revisiting whether the axes could consolidate") sometimes lands in a fact bullet rather than `ourTake`, which itself may still read as general industry commentary and worth an elevation to pull the personal-stakes line up into `ourTake` directly.
- **2026-07-13** — In this pipeline's outline shape, the "line that lands" reliably shows up already-formed in the second `turn` beat's `ourTake` (the "one gate that isn't a model" tone-gate beat) — an aphoristic closer like "The gate I trust most is the one with no opinion." Don't rewrite it; flag it as an elevation to *protect* verbatim through drafting (e.g. append a preserve-this-line note to that beat's `gateGuidance`), since sanding/paraphrasing during drafting is the likelier failure mode than the outline lacking one.
- **2026-07-13** — Same sanding risk shows up one beat earlier than the tone-gate turn: the synthesis/disposition beat's `ourTake` (the loop-cap paragraph) often carries a personified stakes line — e.g. "it would rather stall than let something broken go out the door looking finished" — that drafting quietly drops, leaving a flatter procedural sentence ("stops the run cold and puts the open findings in front of me") with the personification gone. Check this beat specifically for a dropped clause, not just the tone-gate beat.
- **2026-07-13 (SUPERSEDED same day)** — Earlier read on the tone-gate turn (order 7) treated H2-heading + closing-body-line duplication of "The gate I trust most is the one with no opinion" as dilution to fix by cutting one occurrence. Downstream editorial record (`docs/product-experts/meta-content-winners.md`, draft-review passes on this same post) settled the opposite: heading + standalone closing-paragraph echo is a deliberate "bookend" device and reads as *stronger*, not diluted — the regression they actually flagged was the line surviving ONLY as the H2 with no body occurrence (see next entry). Do NOT flag the heading+closer double-occurrence as dilution; it's the desired state. Only flag if the line is missing from either location entirely.
- **2026-07-13** — Opposite variant of the above, seen in the same beat: drafting sometimes drops the protected line from the body prose entirely, leaving it ONLY as the section's H2 heading with no occurrence in the paragraph text at all. Since a heading isn't prose the reader "lands" on mid-read, this still counts as the flare line missing — flag as an elevation to insert the line verbatim into the body (e.g. right after the sentence establishing the gate can't be argued down), not just present in the heading.
- **2026-07-13** — In the synthesis/disposition beat (order 6, the loop-cap paragraph), the personified stakes line ("it would rather stall... than let something broken go out the door looking finished") survived intact this round — that risk (see precedent above) isn't universal. But the beat's closing sentence, which in the outline's `ourTake` reads in first person with a relief payoff ("I don't have to referee fifteen conflicting opinions myself — a function does, and that's the whole point of building it this way"), got flattened in drafting to a third-person procedural restatement ("A function referees the fifteen conflicting opinions instead of me") — losing both the "I" and the "that's the whole point of building it this way" payoff clause. Watch this exact closing sentence specifically for that flattening in future drafts of this beat.
