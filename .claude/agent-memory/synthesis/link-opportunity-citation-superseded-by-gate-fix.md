Recurred 2026-07-15, "dark-dashboard-design" outline, rounds 1, 2, 3, and 4 —
same span every time (order 6, the series-color/colorblind claim).

`link-opportunity` keeps proposing to *source* an unsourced design-intent
claim (e.g. "series colors are colorblind-validated" / "deliberately
avoiding red/green") by adding a citation URL. `factual-accuracy` and
`technical-honesty` (gate tier) fixed the same span in round 2 by removing or
softening the unsourced-intent framing itself (landed as "which also happens
to sidestep red/green" — a coincidental-fact framing, not a claimed
deliberate design choice) rather than sourcing it. Once that gate fix lands,
there's no more "claim" left that needs a citation.

**Round 3 update: the ask recurred a third time with NO active gate fix on
the span that round** — `link-opportunity` re-proposed the identical WebAIM
citation against the already-de-claimed, round-2-landed text, unprompted by
any fresh gate finding. This means the pattern isn't only a same-round
gate-fix collision artifact; it's a standing low-value ask on this specific
span that the reviewer keeps re-raising regardless of whether the span was
touched that round. Treat it as permanently superseded, not just
round-of-collision-superseded.

**Round 4 confirms this is now a stable per-reviewer pattern, not a fluke**:
`link-opportunity` raised the identical WebAIM-citation ask a fourth
consecutive round, wording unchanged, target span unchanged, still no active
gate fix on order 6 that round. Four-for-four. No further re-evaluation
needed on this specific span — drop it immediately on sight without spending
synthesis reasoning cycles re-deriving why.

the `every rendered link resolves` axis (gate tier) flagged the inline WebAIM

superseded ask, not just `link-opportunity` alone.

`factual-accuracy`, or `technical-honesty`) targeting the series-color/

citation-add ask surfaces on a *different* span, evaluate fresh; this
precedent is scoped to this specific claim, not citation-add elevations
generally.
